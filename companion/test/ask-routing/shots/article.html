Why sleep helps you remember what you studied

By Dana Okafor, 6 min read, updated March 3

Researchers have known for decades that people remember new facts better after a night of sleep than after the same number of hours awake. A study of 120 first-year students found that those who slept at least seven hours after a study session recalled 21 percent more word pairs the next morning.

The reason is replay. During deep sleep the brain repeats the patterns it formed during the day and moves them from short-term storage into long-term memory. Cutting sleep short mostly cuts the last part of the night, which is when much of this replay happens.

The practical advice is simple: study the hardest material in the evening, avoid all-nighters before an exam, and keep a regular bedtime in exam week. A 20 minute nap after a morning study block also helped, but less than a full night.
